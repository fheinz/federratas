---
title: "The Chosen One"
date: 2006-06-18
categories: ["Atentados paraliterarios", "Ellas"]
---

She didn't deserve this kind of disappointment. After all, she was not
one of those floozies who recklessly embark on adventures doomed to
failure. Not at all; she carefully chose the men she got involved
with, and yet they always managed to let her down.

She had met Marcos at a barbecue at Pablo's house. It wasn't much of a
reference, because all the men in that group had ended up disppointing
her one way or another. But at least there was a formal introduction,
and she took advantage of that very day to make the first inquiries
disguised as after-dinner small talk. In this way, she learned of his
engineering career, his work at a car factory, his taste for cinema,
classical music, martial arts, astronomy, and Chinese food. At the
same time, she was pleased with his good table manners, detected a
slight tendency to stutter that gave him an endearing air of
vulnerability, and observed with satisfaction that he ate with
moderation, did not abuse proteins, did not smoke, did not stare at
her breasts while talking to her, kept his nails short and clean, that
his gums did not stain the apple with blood when biting it, that he
wore no cologne, and that the roots of his hair were the same color as
the tips.

If she had rushed at this stage, if she had been satisfied with what
he had to say and show of himself, her misfortune would have been more
understandable. But she already had too many rodeos under her belt to
be so naive.

She had to wait until the next day to call the factory where he said
he worked. The operator connected her to the extension, and she hung
up immediately upon hearing him say "hello." Once his job was
confirmed, she dedicated the rest of the week to sifting through
university archives until she found proof that his engineering degree
was genuine, and verified that he had graduated without any failed
courses.

She was fully aware that a man is more than a job and a title. There
are secrets hiding behind every man, she thought, so she hired her
usual private detective to uncover them. He was a meticulous, serious
professional, so trustworthy that she might have considered him
suitable for a relationship beyond the professional, were it not for
his habit of calling himself "Marlowe." After a few days, Marlowe
delivered the usual report, detailing Marcos' favorite readings, as
well as his marital and financial status, vices, previous romantic
relationships, political orientation, bank accounts at home and
abroad, membership in clubs, brotherhoods, and lodges, medical
history, sports activities, religious beliefs, traffic fines from the
last five years, musical aptitudes, reprimands received in high
school, debts, hygiene habits, and frequency of visits to his mother.

She was starting to feel good about herself. Thinking about how much
her future children would thank her for the lengths she had gone to in
order to choose a suitable father, she took a trial class at the same
aikido institute where he practiced, with the sole objective of
scratching his skin with the sampling ring Marlowe had procured for
her. It was just eniugh for her to get a DNA analysis that confirmed
the absence of congenital diseases, ruled out any tendency toward
diabetes or cancer, and yielded a very encouraging initial diagnosis
regarding their mutual genetic compatibility.

Finally, she located and visited each and every one of his previous
girlfriends, posing as an Avon representative. She put the result of
these interviews through a delicately balanced evaluation process:
these were women he had chosen, so she had to ensure they were sound
of mind, because she had no interest in falling for a man who liked
airheads. But at all times she kept in mind that these were also women
he had rejected, and therefore she had to pinpoint some defect or flaw
that justified such a step, exempting him from the cruelty of having
left them without a valid reason.

Recapping her actions, she could find no fault in them. She had done
everything in her power to ensure that her relationship with Marcos
had the best chances of success. Only then had she begun to devise
plans to casually cross paths with him in some situation conducive to
him inviting her to dinner, or better, to the movies. She had long
evaluated what to wear for the first date, would the little black
dress be better than the burgundy outfit? She had even wondered if he
would agree to her plan to marry outside the city, since she always
thought that the small chapel in Candonga was way prettier than any
urban church. Everything was going perfectly to plan until the moment
when, while composing in her mind a future family photograph with
their three children and him (now a bit older and sporting a husbandly
paunch of satisfaction), she suddenly remembered that, that night at
Pablo's, she hadn't liked Marcos at all.
