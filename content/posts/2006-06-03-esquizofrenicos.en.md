---
title: "Schizophrenics"
date: 2006-06-03
categories: ["Atentados paraliterarios", "Ellas"]

You probably know a few of those men that trade on the Market for
Bachelors, Divorcees, and the Like. Think about them for a moment;
recall their attitudes, appearances, outfits, hairstyles, and
ideologies of the last few months (or hours, in particularly severe
cases). You will likely conclude that these individuals are
somewhat... shall we say...  volatile. It is as if being on the loose
turned them into flip-floppers that would put a chameleon to shame. Is
it true that the male needs the female to be even remotely stable, as
my grandmother used to decree?

Nothing further from my spirit than to question my granny's authority
in these matters, of course, but let's look into the situation more in
detail before we go all out using gasoline to put out fires. What ails
these poor wretches is not some deficiency of womanine in the
bloodstream. The inconstancy we see is just the consequence of their
pathetic efforts to achieve an image close to what they glimpse as
"the woman's ideal." Yet, no matter how much they writhe, contradict
and torture themselves, they fail. Which, on the other hand, might be
for the best.

Let's attempt a description of the modern woman's "ideal man": he is
fun, original, and creative, and at the same time a good provider, an
exemplary worker who is never five minutes late to the job at the
bank. He is sensitive, he tears up (out of emotion, not disgust)
listening to Luis Miguel sing "El Día que me Quieras". He has the
emotional stability and strength to rebuild the self-esteem of a piece
of chewing gum stuck under a table. He never contradicts her in what
she desires, and knows how to "force" her to do what she secretly
wants but dares not admit. He is successful in his work, highly sought
after for his professional skills, and whistles a happy tune while, he
cooks, washes the dishes, does the laundry, and cleans the house. He
is kind, considerate, knows how to listen, and is a debauched, wild
animal in bed. He is desired by many women, but he doesn't even see
them. He is an exquisite dancer of Latin, European, Indo-Asian, and
African rhythms; he knows how to fix every household appliance ever
devised and is capable of building a house with his own hands. He
dedicates a lot of time to the relationship, is always there when she
needs him, and vanishes with a flash of light and a faint blue puff of
smoke when she needs her space. He is faithful to the point of
exasperation, never giving her the slightest grounds for jealousy, and
understands that if she ever has a little fling with no more
consequence than a pair of twins with hard-to-justify genetics, it's
only because she's suffocating and needs some air.

Look at them, stampeding toward the sunset in pursuit of this elusive
character, leaving our friends trampled, mired in a trail of dust and
their own ruminations. Thinking about it, the troubling bit is not
that they may be seeking someone like that. What's really terrifying
is the remoyte possibility that they might actually find him. Because
this guy is a nutjob that makes Norman Bates look like an impassive
English butler. Sooner or later, this lunatic is going to end up as a
serial killer, howling at the Moon from the rooftop of a building with
the Names of the Beast tattooed on his naked body. People like that
are the raw material for Stephen King novels.

If I may be allowed the poor taste of being honest, I will admit that
men also tend to fall into the trap of polymorphic desire, usually
embarking on the search for women who are a "mixture of saint and
whore."  Generally, it is inexperienced youths who ignore the wisdom
of our elders, the long tradition of exemplary men who lived highly
satisfactory lives with a saint and a whore (or two), neatly separated
and never mixing them up. These great men teach us to appreciate the
delights of each: one efficient, the other warm; one serene, the other
eccentric; one with skills in knitting, the other adept at the art of
chest-to-chest massage, each one comfortable in her own ecological
niche.

By the way, it's not enough to avoid looking for her: women are
unpredictable phenomena, and men finds themselves, perhaps too
frequently, entangled with one who aspires to embody this indigestible
combination. Fortunately, they are usually harmless if one takes the
precaution of not being the one for which she plays the saint.
