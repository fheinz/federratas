---
title: "Nature is unfair"
date: 2006-06-11
categories: ["Atentados paraliterarios", "Ellas"]
---

When I was a child, already old enough to know I was a boy but not yet
old enough to have invented woman, I yearned to grow up quickly, to
become an adult man. And how could I not long for that, considering
the promise of my genetic destiny? Growing up male guaranteed that,
eventually, inevitably, I would stop being a frightened little animal
and become practically a superhero. I would be part of the Stronger
Sex; I would be solid and impassive like a rock in a storm; I would be
the fulcrum, the point of reference, the paradigm of truth and justice
for those around me.

Time flowed parsimoniously in those years. Today, it seems to have
bolted into a homicidal race, of which I seem to be the victim, and I
find myself approaching (in the wrong direction) an age at which my
slow metamorphosis from worm to King of Creation should have been long
complete&hellip; and instead of celebrating, I find myself reflecting
on the sad state of the monarchy in our century.

I admit that, in my particular case, this disillusionment stems at
least in part from unavoidable facts. It's no secret that I ended up
becoming something much closer to Woody Allen than to Tarzan. Not only
did I not become the the Ape-Man's handsome brother, I fear that our
closest kinship dates back to some weird slug species which eventually
evolved into shrews.  But beyond these personal vicissitudes, I always
suspected the existence of a more generalized masculine malaise, one
which all men suffer when they face the disparity between that
dazzling cultural image and our daily reality.

I made inquiries among my peers and, indeed, the clamor was unanimous.
From a statistical point of view, this uniformity of opinion is
significant because men, as a group, have very little in common with
each other. So little, in fact, that we do not hesitate to openly
celebrate our differences through brawls, football matches, insults,
wars, and other activities broadly classified under the heading of
"sports." However, on one complaint we all agree: Nature has treated
us very harshly.

A dispassionate look at our species' means of reproduction is enough
to realize that our function in Natural History is limited to mingling
the genes of different women. We are the wooden spoon that the female
Cooks of Creation use to stir the genetic soup every now and
then. Seen this way, it is immediately recognizable that men are
basically a genetic experiment managed by women. Our bodies are mere
life-support systems for the aforementioned "spoon," and our
rudimentary intellect seems to be the only thing that went wrong: the
plan was for us to have none at all. When it comes down to it, a penis
is the means by which one woman produces another woman. Nothing more,
nothing less

Once we have establised our place in Nature's hierarchy, it's not
surprising we have become primitive and melancholic beings. After all,
no-one needs men to contribute great works, beauty, or glory: as long
as we deliver 23 chromosomes in a relatively good state of
preservation, we've fulfilled our mission. Better yet, we must provide
them often and abundantly, so they don't go to waste. To this end,
Mother Nature's wisdom took great care to omit any sense of shame in
the design of our minds, or at least to create a mechanism that
disables it as soon as we intend to approach a woman. If we had any
vestige of self-respect, would we be capable of hiding our homicidal
impulse (and even feign interest!) when we are instructed for the
umpteenth time on the supposed vices and virtues of our zodiac sign?

To complete the affront, the inadequate substitute our mothers gave us
by way of a psyche is so fragile that the threatening shadow of our
looming senile impotence frightens us from age 13, to the point of
secretly hoping we'll have the good taste and foresight to accumulate
enough cholesterol to provoke a well-timed heart attack that takes us
while we still can get it up, sparing us the embarrassment.

We men have very few, almost no reasons to be happy about our lot. But
believe me, despite all of this, we are still way better off than
women. Men are, yes, the most abstruse members of the animal
kingdom. But imagine them, poor things, with nothing else to fall in
love with but us!
