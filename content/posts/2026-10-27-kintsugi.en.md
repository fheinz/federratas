The future, as the song goes, is a foreign land.

Symmetry demands that, from the present, the past be one too.

Thirteen years have passed since this blog's last blunder, and more than
six since it fell off the grid. As tempting as it would be to resume
with "as we were saying yesterday," I'm a far cry from the enviable
(and surely apocryphal) consistency of Fray Luis de León.

Resurrecting it was a technical journey, but also a professional and,
above all, a personal one.

Let’s take them one by one.

## The Technical Journey

Getting this site back online involved many steps. I fortuitously
discovered a long-forgotten partial backup. I scoured the Wayback
Machine's API. I rescued articles from copies generously shared by
others on websites and mailing lists. I returned to programs and
formats that once lived in my muscle memory but are now a vague
recollection. I searched for modern alternatives to the old stack. I
re-encountered the eternal, futile struggle to bend software to my
will.

The result is imperfect. Some images seem to be lost forever, and I
doubt I will be able to reconstruct them. Footnotes, captions, and
other elements were in formats that even WordPress no longer supports,
and looked hideous. I spent considerable time converting them, but I’m
not sure I’ve caught them all. Many hyperlinks point to resources that
no longer exist. I did what I could; it will have to do.

It's a good application of an idea I should have embraced long ago:
there is beauty in imperfection.

## The Professional Journey

The irony of calling a static website a "modern alternative" is not lost
on me. The '90s called; they want their technology back.

The choice reflects changes both in myself and the industry.

As far as I’m concerned, I've grown to appreciate simplicity as a
value of its own. Life is too short to spend it tracking the latest
vulnerabilities and updates for a dynamic website's complex software
stack.  Yes, managing and programming computers is my profession, but
I don’t come here to work.

Of course, the industry now offers alternatives where others,
technically far more competent than me, handle the administration. But
if you ever see me moving this site to a "platform" (Instagram,
Facebook, whatever, I studiously and deliberately ignore them), it
will be a clear sign that aliens have seized control of my body, and
the most merciful thing would be to put me out of my misery.

Beyond infrastructure choices, bringing this site online confronted me
with technical blunders written ten or twenty years ago.

It’s amusing to read detailed, complex instructions on [how to achieve
technical effects that are trivial today]({{< ref
"2010-06-16-reconfiguracion-automagica-de-monitores.md" >}}). Or to see
devices that are now commonplace [characterized as science fiction]({{<
ref "2010-05-01-patentando-el-cielo.md" >}}) (fortunately without
invalidating the argument, as that's what they were at the time).

It's far more painful to face the naïveté of the Free Software
movement's techno-socio-political vision at the time. The fact
that it was shared by many does not excuse my part in it.

We were convinced that the tools we were building would lead to a
better, freer, more egalitarian, more democratic world. We were so
busy improving our toys that we failed to notice when they became the
raw material to build corporations that led us to a world at the
antipodes of our Utopia.

I still agree with my observations from those times. Free Software
undoubtedly won the battle. I have serious doubts, however, as to
whether it was the right battle to fight.

From all this comes a return to the basics. A personal website. No
social networks (the worst idea of our age). Proudly Web&nbsp;1.0. I
am unlikely to add comments. The original site had them, and there was
some mildly interesting debate, but I have grown skeptical of
conversations where people pretend to talk to each other while
actually writing for the gallery.

Not that I expect much of a gallery anyway. I used to have an
audience; I doubt anyone who reads this now isn't a personal friend,
someone who can chat with me over coffee without needing to post
comments.

## The Personal Journey

The most stirring part of the journey was, no doubt about it, hearing
the voice of who I used to be decades ago. I felt overwhelmed with
tenderness for that angry, impatient man, plagued by insecurities,
hiding doubts and anguish behind sarcasm and unshakeable convictions.

I can't help but smile thinking how much my tenderness would piss him
off.

Hearing his voice, I realize that I love him more than he loves
himself. I watch him mask the wounded child inside him, forge
certainties in search of safety, haggle for the affections that might
sustain him, hope that external validation will be enough to
compensate for his lack of self-compassion and self-love.

He doesn't yet know that none of it will work. That catastrophic
ruptures lie ahead, that he will have no choice but to reassemble
himself from whatever shards he finds scattered on the ground (and
don't you miss the parallel with the genesis of this site!).

He hasn't yet experienced what it feels like to not be
depressed. Worse, he doesn't even know that he doesn't know. He hasn't
discovered that he can *choose* to feel better. That it's actually
possible for his inner critic to *shut the fuck up*.

Coexisting with him in this space (with them, actually, since there is
more than one of him, as there is of me) is not a decision I took
lightly. I could have gone *tabula rasa* and started from scratch with
my current voice, catering to the pernicious illusion of finality that
leads us to believe that who we are today is who we were always meant
to be, only to realize how wrong we were but a few years later.

I won't hold back from correcting his texts if I can improve on the
style. Nor will I hesitate to discard some of them if I deem them
irrelevant today. But I want him here, by my side, with his and my
beauties, contradictions and flaws in plain sight. Because I love him,
because I love myself, and I hope to remember to love us in the
future.

## To infinity, and beyond!

I have no idea where this site will go from here. I have no delusion
or desire of turning it into anything big. Quite the opposite; what
prompted me to launch it is the desire for a place to share with
people I love, without having to send a direct message to each one.

The content will be, I imagine, eclectic: travel notes, reflections,
book or movie reviews. I don't even rule out the possibility of having
an original idea, eventually.

In any case, here we are. The day is beautiful, and Autumn is
beginning to paint the slopes of the Kyushu mountains with shades of
red and yellow. Stay with me, let's play together.
