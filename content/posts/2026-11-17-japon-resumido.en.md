I'm well aware I have no idea what I'm talking about, but this tree
may be a good summary of how I see Japan and the Japanese.

Look at it. Isn't it beautiful? Doesn't it convey a sense of peace and
calm?

The thing is: that tree isn't naturally like that. That's not the
shape the tree "wants" for itself. If they had left it to its own
devices, it would have a different canopy. It would be bushy, wild.

But they didn't leave it in peace. Throughout its whole life, it has
been repeatedly and meticulously pruned, and they're still doing it
today. They only allowed those branches to grow that sprouted at
favorable angles and heights to produce the *ma* (empty, blank space)
that is the hallmark of Japanese aesthetics. And even those branches
were forced into shape using ropes, stakes and stones until they were
fixed in the perfect position.

That beauty, that peace, are quite literally the result of decades of
systematic torture.

I can't help but draw a parallel with the vibe I get (admittedly, as
an outsider looking in) from Japanese society. A society where
everything seems to work, where everyone seems to have their place,
where there's almost zero friction for anything, where it's
practically impossible to get fired from your job, where there's free
healthcare&hellip; all as long as you stay in your lane, stomach your
boss's abuse, do pointless, unproductive and unpaid overtime, and
don't even think about doing things your own way.

The metaphor of this tree moves me deeply, on at least two levels.

On an individual level, there's the compassion for the Japanese
people. Having grown up in Argentina, the idea of being abused by my
native culture isn't exactly foreign to me, but this shit is next
level.

I know it's not contemporary, but the image of the young kamikaze
pilots, forced to write poetry about how the cherry flowers dazzle
with their beauty and short life long before other trees even get to
blossom, comes to mind.

Or that of the [boy standing by the
crematory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boy_Standing_by_the_Crematory)
after the Nagasaki atomic bomb. The picture is heartbreaking enough on
its own, but what drove that kid, carrying his baby brother's corpse
on his back, to prefer biting his lower lip until it bled rather than
letting out the tears that were clearly fighting to escape? How broken
was that kid already, even before the Yanks delivered the coup de
grâce?

On a collective level, compassion for Japanese society, and the
question of what the hell must have happened to them to make them
choose to operate this way. Because it must have served them somehow,
and still does. And a lot of them seem to like it, although it's hard
to take someone at their word when you know one of their core values
is to never reveal their true feelings.
