---
title: "Take note, New Zealanders: I envy your professional IT organization"
date: 2013-05-09
categories: ["Efimérides", "El resto"]
---

It is a matter of public record that the United States has long been
employing tactics worthy of a playground bully. They want the entire
world to swallow the same [software
patents](http://patentabsurdity.com/) that are currently stifling
innovation on their own turf.

Many nations (the European Union included) choose not to cross them.
However, the comparatively tiny state of New Zealand has just proposed a
law that [explicitly excludes software from the realm of
patentability](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/sop/government/2013/0237/latest/096be8ed80ae061a.pdf).

The interesting bit: the bill enjoyed the input and backing of the
[Institute of IT
Professionals](http://www.iitp.org.nz/newsletter/article/430). In
Argentina, the software patent issue has been discussed more than once
within the context of [SADIO](http://www.sadio.org.ar). Yet, we never
came to a definitive stance on the matter.

How lovely it must be to have a professional organization that actually
concerns itself with things that matter!
