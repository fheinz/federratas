---
title:
  "Listen up, New Zealanders: I envy your Institute of IT Professionals"
date: 2013-05-09
categories: ["Efimérides", "El resto"]
---

It's well-known that the United States has long been using tactics
befitting a playground bully to ensure that the same [software
patents](http://patentabsurdity.com/) are accepted worldwide, patents
that are hindering innovation even within their own borders.

Many countries (including the European Union) choose not to oppose them,
but the comparatively tiny nation of New Zealand has just proposed a law
that [clearly excludes software from being
patentable](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/sop/government/2013/0237/latest/096be8ed80ae061a.pdf).

What's interesting about this: the bill was supported and endorsed by
the [Institute of IT
Professionals](http://www.iitp.org.nz/newsletter/article/430). In
Argentina, the issue of software patents has been raised several times
within the context of [SADIO](http://www.sadio.org.ar), but there has
never been a clear position taken on the matter.

How wonderful it must be to have a professional organization that deals
with important issues!
