---
title: "Could it be that we aren't as far apart as we think?"
date: 2013-10-13
categories: ["Efimérides", "El resto"]
---

I've just finished reading an article by [Ana
Montes](http://asociacionmigre.com.ar/socio/AnaMontes), “[The victim
showed up for the
date](http://asociacionmigre.com.ar/mirada-de-autor/la-victima-acudio-a-la-cita_5),”
in which she recounts her attendance at [a Fundación Vía Libre
event](http://www.vialibre.org.ar/2013/10/04/hacia-una-agenda-propositiva-para-reformar-el-derecho-de-autor/),
during which [Julio Raffo](http://www.julioraffo.com/) presented his
[bill to reform Law
11.723](http://www.julioraffo.com/index.php/cultura/353-raffo-presenta-en-flacso-su-proyecto-de-acceso-a-la-cultura-en-el-espacio-publico-internet).
It's a project of which I hope I am not entirely innocent: I harbor the
illusion that my chats with Julio might have contributed to its
gestation, and he was kind enough to share the early drafts with me.

Given that the title of the piece makes it crystal clear that Ana feels
like a victim of the project, and that she writes on behalf of La Migré,
I waded into the text expecting the usual, mind-numbing, and
ill-informed whining that without draconian copyright laws, authors
would promptly starve to death. I was not disappointed: there they were,
in all their glory, those and many other misguided clichés—like the one
claiming copyright, as we know it, is a human right ([spoiler: it
isn't](http://www.bea.org.ar/2012/01/el-derecho-de-autor-desde-los-derechos-humanos/)).

However, halfway through the article, I stumbled upon this sentence,
highlighted by the author herself:

> We authors are in favor of the free circulation of content on the web,
> and the only one who should pay is whoever negotiates with our desires
> to create and be re-created.

The sheer difficulty of reconciling this notion with the rest of the
article isn't enough to dampen the joy of finding a common ground: yes,
we authors *are* in favor of the free circulation of works[^1] on the
web (and elsewhere). And that is precisely why we must support the
reform of Law 11.723, because that law *is one of the most restrictive on
the planet and actively conspires against the circulation of works.*

Welcome then, Ana (and perhaps La Migré with her?) to the reformist
ranks of 11.723. We surely won't see eye to eye on everything, but if we
truly favor the free circulation of works, we must at least agree that
the law needs a serious loosening up.

The rest is just a matter of details.

[^1]:
    I understand why industries talk about "content": for those who
    manufacture containers (books, CDs, DVDs), "content" is merely the
    excuse to sell them. To a bottle manufacturer, wine is "content,"
    but I don't think we authors should embrace this relegation of our
    work to the background.
